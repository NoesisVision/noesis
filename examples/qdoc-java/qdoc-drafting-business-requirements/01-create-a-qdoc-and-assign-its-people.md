# QDoc drafting 1: Create a QDoc and assign its people

Part 1 of 8 of the QDoc drafting business requirements, split so that each
part can be delivered in order and is useful on its own. Prepared from the
domain discovery session between the domain expert and the analyst; see
[the overview](README.md) for why the system exists, the roles, the whole
lifecycle and what is out of scope.

**Builds on:** nothing; this is the first part.

**Delivers:** a quality manager (or an auditor) starts a controlled document
in the system: it gets a number, version 1 and an empty content file, the
right people are on it, the other quality managers know about it, and the
activity list shows who did what and when.

## Vocabulary

**QDoc (quality document).** A procedure, guideline, instruction, policy or
similar document that the organisation must maintain. A QDoc is not a single
file. It is a package that always functions as a whole, like a bank loan
agreement with its annexes: signing the agreement means signing everything in
the package. A QDoc has a title, a type, a document number and one or more
versions. It is either active or archived.

**Document type.** Procedure, guideline, instruction, policy. The list may
grow.

**Document number (signature).** A human-facing identifier generated by the
system when the QDoc is created. Used for searching and for referring to the
document in conversation. It is not a security device and has nothing to do
with proving the content was not tampered with; that comes later, at
publication.

**Version.** A complete, self-contained edition of a QDoc. A version is what
goes through review, approval and publication. Versions carry the workflow
status; the QDoc itself does not. A version contains files.

**Content file.** A file the authors write in a rich-text editor. This is the
body of the document. A version must always have at least one content file.
Comments and edit history attach to content files.

**Activity list.** The per-document log of everything that happened to it:
who created it, who was assigned, who edited which file, who commented, who
approved, when. Comparable to the activity tab of an issue tracker.

**Quality manager.** The person responsible for the whole document lifecycle
in the organisation. Creates QDocs, assigns people to them, creates new
versions. Does not write content.

**Author (also called contributor).** The only role that may edit content
files and add attachments. Responsible for preparing the document. The expert
used "author" and "contributor" interchangeably and asked to standardise on
"author".

**Reviewer.** Reads the shared version and leaves comments. Optional; a
document may have none. Reviewers do not block the process. In the future a
reviewer may be an AI agent rather than a person.

**Approver.** Takes responsibility for the document by approving each of its
files. At least one is required before a version can be shared. Approvers may
close comments and, in the end, must approve; they cannot reject a document
outright.

**Auditor.** An external, accredited person who periodically inspects the
quality process. May create test documents in the system to watch the process
run.

## Who may do it

| Role | Creates QDocs | Assigns people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quality manager | yes | yes |
| Auditor | yes (test documents) | yes, on own documents |
| Author | no | no |
| Reviewer | no | no |
| Approver | no | no |
| Employee | no | no |

- Only the quality manager may add authors. An author who wants a colleague
  on the document asks the quality manager outside the system; there is no
  in-system request. The expert initially allowed authors to add contributors,
  then reversed that during the session.
- The auditor is treated like a quality manager for their own documents. The
  difference is only in how the document number is formed and in the
  convention that such documents are published to a very narrow audience.
- Anyone assigned to a document may be a real person or, in the future, an
  automated actor (an AI reviewer, the system itself when it removes a file
  that failed scanning). The activity list must make clear which actions were
  taken by the system rather than by a person.

## Creating a QDoc

The quality manager learns, by whatever means, that a new document is needed
and creates it in the system. The session considered and rejected the
alternative where the quality manager merely tells an author to go and create
it; the skeleton is always created by the quality manager (or auditor).

To create a QDoc the creator supplies:

- a title (required),
- a document type (required),
- at least one author (required),
- reviewers (optional, can be added later),
- approvers (optional at creation, but see [Assigning people](#assigning-people)).

When the QDoc is created the system automatically:

- generates the document number,
- creates version 1 with status new,
- creates the first, empty content file inside version 1 for the authors to
  fill in,
- records the creation in the activity list,
- notifies every quality manager in the organisation that a new document
  exists. Creating a procedure is a significant event and the other quality
  managers should know about it.

Until the version is shared for review, it is visible only to the quality
manager who created it and to the assigned authors.

## The document number

Every QDoc receives a number of the form `<sequence>/<month>/<year>`, for
example `1/12/2025` for the first document created in December 2025. The
sequence part increases by one with each new document. The month and year are
the month and year of creation. The expert compared it to invoice numbering.

The organisation's own prefix or suffix, the `demo` prefix and the auditor
suffix come in [part 2](02-customise-document-numbers.md).

## Assigning people

The quality manager may add authors, reviewers and approvers at any time
while the document is not archived. Every assignment records who was assigned,
by whom and when in the activity list, and the assigned person should be
notified.

Rules that apply to every assignment, whichever role:

- The person doing the assigning must be the quality manager (or the auditor
  for their own document).
- The assigned person must exist in the system, must hold the role they are
  being assigned to, and must be an active user. A blocked or deactivated
  account cannot be assigned.
- The document must not be archived.
- If all conditions hold, the person appears on the document's list for that
  role; if not, the assignment is refused. The session noted the refusal does
  not need a polished user experience, since it mostly arises from misuse or
  from a role being withdrawn between the assignment and its acceptance.

The session discussed an invitation model, as used by GitHub, where the
assigned person receives an invitation and the checks run when they accept it.
This was left as one of several ways to meet the rules above, all of which
must give the same business outcome.

## Activity list and notifications

The activity list of a document records, from this part: the creation and
every assignment (who, by whom, when). Status transitions must not get lost;
somebody reading the list later should never be surprised that something
happened without a trace.

Notifications required:

- All quality managers in the organisation when a QDoc is created.
- A person when they are assigned to a document (implied by the invitation
  discussion; the exact form was not settled).

## Scenarios

**Standard document, first steps.** The quality manager of a software company
creates "Web application testing procedure", type procedure, with one author.
The system numbers it `1/12/2025`, creates version 1 with one empty content
file, and tells the other quality managers.

**Second author.** An author realises the document needs a colleague's input.
They ask the quality manager, who adds the colleague as an author. The
colleague must already hold the author role and have an active account;
otherwise the assignment is refused. The addition appears in the activity
list with the date and the quality manager's name.

## Open questions
