# QDoc drafting 2: Customise document numbers

Part 2 of 8 of the QDoc drafting business requirements, split so that each
part can be delivered in order and is useful on its own. Prepared from the
domain discovery session between the domain expert and the analyst; see
[the overview](README.md) for why the system exists, the roles, the whole
lifecycle and what is out of scope.

**Builds on:** [part 1](01-create-a-qdoc-and-assign-its-people.md), which
numbers every QDoc `<sequence>/<month>/<year>`.

**Delivers:** an organisation's numbers fit its own conventions and never
collide with, say, its invoice numbers; a document from a demo installation
or an auditor's test document can never be mistaken for a binding one. This
part does not depend on the later ones and may be delivered at any point
after part 1.

## Vocabulary

**Document number (signature).** A human-facing identifier generated by the
system when the QDoc is created. Used for searching and for referring to the
document in conversation. It is not a security device and has nothing to do
with proving the content was not tampered with; that comes later, at
publication.

**Auditor.** An external, accredited person who periodically inspects the
quality process. May create test documents in the system to watch the process
run.

## Requirements

Every QDoc receives a number of the form `<sequence>/<month>/<year>`, for
example `1/12/2025` for the first document created in December 2025. The
sequence part increases by one with each new document. The month and year are
the month and year of creation. The expert compared it to invoice numbering.

Customers frequently have their own conventions. They may want a prefix or
suffix so that document numbers do not collide with, say, their invoice
numbers. The number template must therefore be configurable per organisation.

On top of the core template, two situational rules apply:

- Documents created in a demo instance of the product carry a `demo` prefix.
  The purpose is to make it visible that a demo is being used, so that nobody
  quietly runs real quality documents through a trial installation.
- Documents created by a user in the auditor role carry an auditor suffix.
  This marks the document as non-binding: it exists so the auditor can watch
  the process, not to become a real procedure.

The auditor is treated like a quality manager for their own documents. The
difference is only in how the document number is formed and in the
convention that such documents are published to a very narrow audience.

## Scenarios

**Audit.** An external auditor logs in to check that the process works. They
create a test procedure; its number carries the auditor suffix. They assign a
member of the audited team as author, approver and eventually the sole
recipient, and watch the document travel through drafting and approval. The
document is real in every respect except its number and, by convention, its
audience. The system does not hide or filter it.

**Demo.** A prospective customer is given a demo installation. Every document
they create is numbered with a `demo` prefix, so a document from the demo can
never be mistaken for a binding one.
